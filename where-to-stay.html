Where to Stay in Kundasang

Kundasang sits on a mountain ridge at about 1,500 metres, at the foot of Mount Kinabalu. The town itself is small — you can drive from one end to the other in five minutes. But the surrounding areas are spread out, and where you stay changes your whole trip.

How Kundasang is laid out

The greater Kundasang area runs roughly northwest to southeast along one main road. At the northwest end is Kinabalu Park — the UNESCO site and where mountain climbers register. About 10 minutes down the road is Pekan Kundasang, the town centre with the market, shops, and the war memorial. Continuing southeast for another 10–15 minutes, you reach the Mesilou highlands — where Desa Dairy Farm sits, and where most of the postcard-view homestays are. Another 20 minutes further east and you're in Ranau, a much bigger town with banks, supermarkets, and budget options — but no view of the mountain. Poring, with its hot springs and rainforest, is about 45 minutes east of Kundasang town.

Most visitors stay in Pekan Kundasang itself or in the Mesilou highlands. Where you choose depends on what you actually want out of your trip.

The five areas

Where travellers actually stay

Each one has a different feel, a different price range, and suits a different kind of traveller.

Pekan Kundasang

Town centre

The heart of Kundasang. Market, restaurants, shops, and the war memorial all within walking distance. Homestays here tend to be mid-range, some with mountain views, some without. The main road can be noisy during the day but quiets down at night.

Best for: travellers without a car, anyone who wants to walk to dinner, first-night stays before heading further up.

Mesilou Highlands

Best views

10–15 minutes south of town, at higher elevation. This is where the classic "wake up to Mount Kinabalu" photos come from. Quiet, cool, and close to Desa Dairy Farm. Most of the higher-end homestays in the area are here. You'll need a car to reach restaurants.

Best for: photographers, couples, families with kids, anyone who came for the view.

Bundu Tuhan

Quiet & forested

A small village about 5 minutes from the Kinabalu Park entrance, surrounded by forest. Cooler than Kundasang town and much quieter. Fewer restaurants, less traffic. Some of the best budget homestays for climbers are here.

Best for: mountain climbers, travellers wanting peace over views, anyone visiting the park early.

Ranau

Bigger town

20 minutes east of Kundasang. A proper Sabah town — banks, supermarkets, hospital, more food options. Accommodation is cheaper and more plentiful. But no Mount Kinabalu view, and you'll be driving back into Kundasang for the attractions.

Best for: budget travellers, longer stays, anyone visiting Poring or the eastern side.

Poring

Rainforest

45 minutes east of Kundasang, at the Poring Hot Springs. Humid, low-altitude rainforest — a completely different environment from the highlands. No mountain view. Very quiet at night. Some eco-lodges and simple stays.

Best for: travellers prioritising the hot springs and canopy walkway, or anyone wanting a rainforest night.

Kinabalu Park area

Inside the park

Lodges inside the UNESCO park itself, near the climbers' registration centre. Expensive, limited, and usually booked months ahead. If you're climbing the mountain, this is where you'll sleep the night before.

Best for: Mount Kinabalu climbers, or travellers who want the park experience without a drive.

Which area is right for you?

First-timer
Mesilou for the view, Pekan Kundasang if you want to walk to dinner.
Photographer
Mesilou. The higher elevation puts you above the morning mist.
Climber
Bundu Tuhan (near the park gate) or Kinabalu Park itself.
Family with kids
Mesilou — close to Desa Dairy Farm, and the view keeps everyone entertained at sunrise.
Budget traveller
Ranau. Cheaper stays, more food options. You'll just need to drive to the sights.
No car
Pekan Kundasang. Walkable food, market, and the war memorial. Hire a driver for day trips.
Hot springs focus
Poring. Stay near the springs and skip the drive back to Kundasang.
Before you book

What to look for in a homestay

  • →Ask for a real photo of the view from the room. "Mountain view" means different things in Kundasang — some rooms see the peak, some see the mountain's shoulder, some see only trees. Ask for a photo taken from the balcony of the specific room you're booking.
  • →Check the bedding. It gets cold at night — 14–18°C is normal. A homestay that looks cheap because there's no heater and only a thin blanket is not a bargain. Ask before you book.
  • →Ask about parking and road access. Some homestays are up steep driveways that a small car can't manage, especially in the rain. If you're driving yourself, confirm the road is paved and the parking is on-site.
  • →Steamboat dinner is a Kundasang tradition. Many homestays offer a communal steamboat dinner in the evening. It's one of the best parts of staying here. Ask if the host arranges it and what it costs.
  • →Don't trust the map pin. Some homestays pin their location at the main road when they're actually 10 minutes up a side road. Ask for the exact location, or check recent reviews.
  • →Check who you're booking with. Every host on Kundasang Homestay is visited in person before they go live. If you're booking elsewhere, look for reviews and confirm the host is reachable on WhatsApp before you pay.
Common questions

Frequently asked

What is the best area to stay in Kundasang?

For first-time visitors and anyone who wants the classic Mount Kinabalu view, the Mesilou highlands (10–15 minutes south of Pekan Kundasang, near Desa Dairy Farm) offer the best combination of view, quiet, and proximity to attractions. Travellers without a car are usually better off in Pekan Kundasang itself, where restaurants, the market, and the war memorial are within walking distance.

Is it better to stay in Kundasang or Ranau?

Kundasang is the better choice for most visitors. It sits at a higher elevation (cooler, with the mountain view) and is closer to the main attractions — Kinabalu Park, Desa Dairy Farm, and the war memorial. Ranau is 20 minutes further east, cheaper, and has better shops and banks, but you will be driving back into Kundasang for everything you came to see.

How far is Kundasang from Kota Kinabalu?

About 90 km, or roughly 2 hours by car. The drive goes through the Crocker Range and is one of the most scenic in Sabah. Most visitors rent a car or arrange a private transfer from Kota Kinabalu. There is no direct public bus to individual homestays, though shared vans run from KK to Ranau.

What is the weather like in Kundasang?

Cool and often misty. Daytime temperatures range from 18–24°C, and nights can drop to 14°C. Rain is common year-round but heaviest from October to February. The clearest mountain views are usually in the early morning — clouds typically roll in by 9–10 AM.

Do I need a car to stay in Kundasang?

Yes, for most areas. Public transport in the highlands is limited to shared vans between towns, and many homestays are up side roads that taxis will not take. If you do not drive, staying in Pekan Kundasang town centre gives you walkable access to food and the main sights, and you can hire a driver for day trips to Poring or Kinabalu Park.
